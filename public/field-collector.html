🎯 What You Are Doing

You are collecting ground-truth data that satellites cannot capture — the real conditions on the ground. Your observations directly feed into Beelove Farm's Kenya Beekeeping Suitability Map. Aim for 8–12 stops per day, spread across different land types and areas.

✅ Signs of High Suitability (7–10)
  • Diverse flowering plants across multiple seasons
  • Dense natural vegetation or healthy mixed smallholder farms
  • Permanent water source within 500m
  • Existing hives with 70%+ colonization rate
  • Low pesticide pressure / no high-risk cash crops nearby
  • No history of frequent absconding
  • Strong swarming season (high trap colonization)
⚠️ Pesticide Risk — What to Look For
  • Ask local farmers what cash crops they grow and how often they spray
  • Citrus (oranges, pixies), tomatoes, French beans = high spray frequency
  • Note any agrochemical stores or dealer signs nearby
  • Dead bees near hive entrances = pesticide poisoning indicator
  • Ask what pesticide brand they use if possible
🐝 African Bee Notes
  • Apis mellifera scutellata absconds when stressed — always ask about absconding history
  • Propolis-baited hives colonize significantly better than wax-baited ones
  • Tree-mounted hives generally colonize better than waist-height
  • Swarming season trap success rate = best indicator of local bee population density
📊 About the Dual Index

The form automatically calculates a suitability score from your field data. You also give your own gut score. When they differ by more than 2 points, add a note — you probably observed something important that the form doesn't capture yet. Those notes help us improve the algorithm.
